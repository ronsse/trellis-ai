## Evidence (current main)

`resolve_recency_stamp` in strategies.py. Chunks inherit `CLASSIFY_METADATA_KEYS` only — no source `created_at`. **6/56 packs** served stamped parent + own chunk; keyword axis now scores them ~2.1× apart (consistency defect introduced by #462, not pre-existing). Owner comment (2026-09): chunk citation split rests on **small n** (7/38 vs 1/64 helpful/unhelpful) — **re-measure before acting**. P(cited helpful|served): parents 0.015, chunks 0.081, corpus 0.123. Those rates are confounded by the import-clock boost under test, so they establish neither that chunks are intrinsically better nor that copying a parent stamp is safe.

## Contradictions

Parent/chunk split new on keyword axis post-#462. A parent conversation's `updated_at` is not per-chunk event time: copying it can make an early span falsely fresh, while copying `created_at` can make a late span falsely old. Unchanged imports also skip chunk rewriting, so write-time inheritance would not repair the motivating rows without a separate backfill.

## Selected solution / decision

Two independent panels agreed to defer the behavior change. The prior ≥30 paired-pack threshold came from this review corpus, not the issue comment, and is not sufficient by itself: it measures exposure to the split, not whether a parent-level stamp is the correct clock for each chunk.

Reopen a behavior change only when at least one stronger condition holds:

1. candidate-set replay shows inheriting parent stamps does not remove cited-helpful chunks relative to a held-out window;
2. ingest preserves per-message/per-span timestamps that can supply an actual chunk event clock; or
3. the owner explicitly defines chunk recency as parent `updated_at`, accepting false freshness for early spans.

A telemetry-only slice may add `parent_doc_id`, selected-stamp age, and recency multiplier to flat pack events without changing ranking.

## Disposition acceptance
